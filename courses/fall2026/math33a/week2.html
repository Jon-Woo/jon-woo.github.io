In the lanauge of matrices (as functions where composition is matrix product) we have the following equivalent defintions of invertibility:

Invertibility of matrices

Let AAA be a m×nm\times nm×n matrix. We can think of AAA as a map from Rn→Rm\R^n \to \R^mRn→Rm. The following are equivalent:

  1. There exists a matrix A−1A^{-1}A−1 such that AA−1x=xAA^{-1}x=xAA−1x=x and A−1Ax=xA^{-1}Ax=xA−1Ax=x. Equivalently AA−1AA^{-1}AA−1 and A−1AA^{-1}AA−1A are the identity matrix in their respective spaces.
  2. For every yyy in Rm\R^mRm, there exists a unique xxx in Rn\R^nRn such that Ax=yAx=yAx=y.
Size of invertible matrices

In the previous definitions, AAA is not necessarily square. It turns out that a non-square matrix cannot be invertible, so let's just assume square matrices from here on out.

Invertibility via homogenous solutions

So we have a bunch of equivalent definitions of invertibility. There are a few more:

More equivalent definitions of invertibility.

The following are equivalent to AAA being invertible:

  1. det⁡(A)=0\det(A)=0det(A)=0 (We will see why in next week, when we do determinants)
  2. AAA is "injective" (this is the uniqueness part in the previous definition; this also somehow implies that n≤mn\leq mn≤m for m×nm\times nm×n matrices, which is to say that the input space is "smaller".)
  3. AAA is "surjective" (this is existence part of the previous definition; this implies m≤nm\leq nm≤n for m×nm\times nm×n matrices, which is to say that the output space is "smaller".)

Note that injectivity and surjectivity force the matrix to be square.
